THE REVOLUTION WILL BE INDEPENDENT.

How Kim Petras broke her chains, built BunHead Records, and created her definitive Y2K masterpiece.

It started with regret. Or maybe it started with frustration. In late 2025, after months of label gridlock, Kim Petras did what few mainstream stars dare to do: she walked away from a major contract. Taking to alternative social channels, she gave her fans a stark, unfiltered update: "They won't let me release what I want. I've waited long enough. I love u guys. I'm dropping Detour regardless."

Then came Polo. Then Freak It. Then I Like Ur Look. Then Need for Speed. Then Jeep — a country-inflected ballad co-written during the LA wildfires that somehow became her most critically praised single yet. And suddenly May 29, 2026 became the most circled date in every pop fan's calendar.

"I wanted it to sound like the music videos I used to watch on MTV in 2004 when I was a kid in Germany dreaming of escape."

Inspired by Gwen Stefani's L.A.M.B., Fergie, The Pussycat Dolls, and M.I.A., Detour represents Kim Petras at maximum creative freedom. She fired a bunch of people. She cut off the label. She worked with trans musicians and collaborators who understood exactly what this album needed to sound like. And the result is, by all accounts, the album of 2026.
